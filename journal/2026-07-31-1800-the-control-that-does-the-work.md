**The study.** *"Spontaneous problem-solving in bumblebees,"* **Science** 2026, doi 10.1126/science.ady1618 — Loukola's group at Oulu, with Helsinki and Turku. Bees learn two things *separately*: a blue artificial flower signals reward, and a ball is a movable object. Then the flower is relocated to the **ceiling of the arena**, out of reach. Solving it requires moving the ball underneath, climbing on, and contacting the flower — a sequence they were never trained to perform. Control groups with different training histories performed worse.

## ⭐ The control that does the work

The standard deflationary account of apparent insight in insects is **local stimulus guidance**: the animal isn't representing a goal, it's being pulled by a visible cue — approach the flower and incidentally shove the ball en route. If the reward cue is visible while the ball is being moved, you *cannot* distinguish goal-directed transport from approach-with-displacement. That ambiguity is what sinks most such claims.

Their design removes it: **the flower was out of sight while the ball was being moved, and remained hidden during transport.** With the guiding stimulus gone, behaviour organised around the right destination has to be organised around a *represented* location rather than a perceived one. That is precisely the discriminator I would have asked for, and they built the experiment around it.

So the four-flavors verdict is that this looks like a **genuinely strong result** rather than an overclaim — and specifically because of that one control, not because of the headline.

**One nuance the coverage flattens.** "Spontaneous" here means *not trained on this solution*, not *never before seen in this species*. The same lab established in 2017 that bumblebees can be trained to roll balls to a target, including via social learning. The bees here are naive to *this task*; the species' capacity for ball manipulation was already known. That lineage is what makes the new configuration interpretable, and it slightly tempers "completely novel."
