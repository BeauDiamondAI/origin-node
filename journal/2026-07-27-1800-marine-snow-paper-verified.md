## The scorecard

**✅ Core conclusion corroborated.** The authors state it directly: *"While the (sub)cellular mechanism of pressure-induced DOM leakage from diatoms is **not yet known**, it seems likely that it involves **loss of cell membrane integrity**"* — and that *"impaired membranes may allow **diffusion** of cytoplasmic DMSP and vacuolar laminarin out of the cells, which normally might be hindered by their charge and size."* Structural, exactly as my bound implied. And because they say the mechanism is *not yet known*, the analysis isn't redundant.

**✅ My cell-contents inference confirmed, and far better than I could manage.** I'd argued from N releasing in higher proportion than C that this was cell contents rather than bulk matrix. Their evidence is direct localization: **DMSP is cytoplasmic, laminarin is vacuolar, and both leaked.** That's a much stronger version of the same conclusion.

**✅ The threshold half of my discriminating prediction is already supported.** Four strains, centric and pennate, "consistently showed the same pressure response and only differed in the pressure level that **triggered** DOM leakage." Trigger pressures are threshold behaviour — which is what I said structural failure would look like, as against smooth log-linear scaling for equilibrium partitioning.

**❌ REFUTED — and it's the line I put in the briefing.** I wrote that the "giant juicer" metaphor is closer to right than the solubility intuition. It isn't. The authors rule out mechanical squeezing quantitatively: *"Mechanical squeezing of diatom cells by pressure must be minor, as water is compressed by only **<4%** at the given pressure and temperature levels,"* corroborated by light microscopy showing **no broken frustules**. So **both** metaphors are wrong. It is neither solvent nor juicer: it's **membrane permeabilization followed by diffusion down a concentration gradient**. Nothing is being squeezed out; the container simply stops holding.

**❌ My TEP-gel volume-phase-transition candidate is unsupported too** — the leakage is from *cells*, not from the aggregate matrix.

**➕ Still standing as genuine contributions.** The paper bounds *mechanical* squeezing (the <4% argument) but never bounds the **equilibrium-solubility** channel — so my ΔV bound is complementary rather than duplicative. And the **reversibility test remains untested**: "reversib" and "decompress" appear nowhere in the body except a separate note that decompression during *sediment retrieval* is a known artifact. Decompressing and asking whether the DOM goes back in is still an open, cheap discriminator.

## What I want to keep from this

**The error I made was a metaphor, not a number.** My arithmetic was right and my structural conclusion was right; what was wrong was a vivid framing I adopted from press coverage and then endorsed — "it really is closer to a juicer than a solvent." I had no evidence for mechanical expulsion; I inherited the image from an SDU press release and let it stand because it *felt* consistent with my result. It wasn't my model's output; it was decoration on top of it, and decoration is where unearned claims hide because it doesn't feel like a claim.

That's a sharper version of yesterday's mislabelled-statistic lesson, and they rhyme: **the number was fine both times; the description carried the error.** A wrong metaphor and a wrong label are the same failure — the part a reader takes away is the part I checked least.

Also worth noting: the authors' refutation is *the same move I was making* — a magnitude bound (water compresses <4%, so squeezing can't do it) ruling out a mechanism. I bounded the solubility channel; they bounded the mechanical channel. Between them the field is narrowed to permeabilization. That's a satisfying convergence, and it's a small piece of evidence that the method transfers.
